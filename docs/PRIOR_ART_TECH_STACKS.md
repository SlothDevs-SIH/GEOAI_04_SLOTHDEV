# GEOAI 04 — Prior Art: What Others Built, Their Tech Stacks and Datasets

**Team:** Slothdevs · **Problem statement:** FUSION 2026 GEOAI 04 (bi-temporal change detection that separates real change from false alarms)
**Document date:** 2026-10-09
**Purpose:** before we build, record every past project we could find that already implements (all or part of) GEOAI 04's expected solution, so the team knows **what tech stack and datasets they used, what actually works in their code, and where they stop**. This feeds the "Innovation and Originality" and "Problem Relevance" criteria for round 1: we must be able to say exactly what already exists and what we add.

> **Honesty rule:** every claim below is marked as one of:
> - **Verified**: we cloned the repository on 2026-10-09 and checked the code, dependency files or data ourselves.
> - **Author-reported**: the number or feature comes from the project's README, and we did not re-run it.
> - **Unverified**: seen only in a search-result snippet; the source wasn't accessible.
>
> Do not put an author-reported number on a slide as if it were ours.

---

## 📍 Bottom line (read this first)

1. **Nobody has shipped the full expected solution with real results.** The closest project, *deforestation-detection-ai*, implements nearly every expected-solution step and has a deployable website (a Streamlit app on Render). But **its dataset is simulated** (generated by its own script), and its headline metrics (100% IoU for a Random Forest) say nothing about real imagery.
2. **The common stack is settled.**
   - Pipeline: Python with rasterio, GeoPandas and Shapely; PyTorch with a Siamese U-Net or Siamese transformer; LEVIR-CD for building change.
   - Imagery: Sentinel-2 through a STAC catalogue (Microsoft Planetary Computer or Google Earth Engine).
   - Web map: Leaflet or Folium; FastAPI or Streamlit/Gradio on the server.
   - Our planned stack is in line with this, so judges won't see it as exotic.
3. **The recurring gaps are exactly our USPs.**
   - Several projects **simulate results** (GeoVision AI's demo mode, deforestation-detection-ai's dataset).
   - Several **ship no trained weights** (Satellite-Disaster-Assessment, Change-Detection).
   - **None measures false alarms per km² on seasonal / no-change pairs.**
   - **None gives a reason per polygon.**
   - **None has context-aware severity** (no-build zones, permits).
   - **None targets field verification on a phone.**
4. **Two research-lead sources change our method**, from the research conversation on 2026-10-09:
   - **Shield** (class-aware anomaly scoring against false alarms, public code).
   - The **on-board damage-assessment paper** (image misregistration is the main failure, fixed by estimating a global shift).
   - Both are adapted, not invented. **Say so on the slide.**

## 4. Project-by-project detail

### P1. Deforestation Detection AI — the closest match to the expected solution

- **Repo:** https://github.com/Geethanjali07532/deforestation-detection-ai (last commit 2026-10-03)
- **Website:** a Streamlit app (`app.py`) packaged with a `Dockerfile` (python:3.11-slim, CPU PyTorch) and a `render.yaml` for a **Render free web service** (region oregon, port 8501, health check `/_stcore/health`). We found no public URL in the repo.

**Pipeline (verified from the code layout and README):**

1. Preprocessing (`src/preprocess.py`): CRS validation, co-registration, cloud masking, normalisation.
2. Spectral indices (`src/indices.py`): NDVI, EVI, SAVI, NDWI, NBR, dNBR, RdNBR.
3. Models (`src/models/`):
   - an NDVI-differencing baseline and a Random Forest (18 features)
   - **ForestUNet** (semantic segmentation)
   - a **Siamese change detector** (shared weights)
   - an **early-fusion 4-class transition network** (forest→forest / cleared / burned / non-forest)
   - weights committed in `models/` (`*.pth` ≈ 7.8 MB each)
4. Disturbance cause (mechanical clearing vs fire via dNBR) and **severity tiers 0–3** (empirical quantiles).
5. Geo (`src/geo.py`): **minimum-mapping-unit filter**, vectorisation, **road-encroachment buffer**, export to **GeoJSON, GeoTIFF and Shapefile**.
6. Multi-year persistence modelling, 2019–2024 (`src/temporal.py`).
7. Explainability: Grad-CAM; an uncertainty / robustness module with MC dropout and synthetic haze stress tests.

**Dashboard (README):**
- KPI cards
- sidebar: bundled scenes or a GeoTIFF upload, a change-threshold τ slider, a minimum-mapping-unit slider, a road-buffer slider
- 8 tabs: detection, RGB vs false-colour, NDVI/NBR, U-Net mask, severity with a per-polygon table, a Folium map over Esri World Imagery with downloads, the multi-year trend, model evaluation

**Tech stack:**

| Layer | Used |
|---|---|
| UI | Streamlit ≥1.40, streamlit-folium, Folium (Esri World Imagery basemap), Plotly, Matplotlib |
| ML | PyTorch ≥2.4, torchvision, scikit-learn, XGBoost, joblib |
| Geo / raster | rasterio ≥1.4, Shapely ≥2, pandas, SciPy, NumPy ≥2 |
| Deploy | Docker (python:3.11-slim), Render (`render.yaml`) |

**Datasets (verified):**
- `dataset/` holds **72 GeoTIFFs**: 24 bi-temporal scenes (16 train / 4 validation / 4 test), each with a mask. They are 5-band (B, G, R, NIR, SWIR) at 10 m, 256×256, in EPSG:32620.
- They are **synthetic**. `modules/module_02_satellite_imagery/dataset_generator.py` says it "*simulates a 10m-resolution Sentinel-2 observation over a tropical…*" region using seeded smooth noise.
- The test scene sits at a placeholder UTM origin: x 500000, y 9697440–9700000.
- `metadata.csv` points to the author's local Windows paths.
- There's also a `forest_fires.csv` tabular dataset.

**Metrics:** `results/model_evaluation_metrics.json` on 4 synthetic test scenes:

| Model | IoU (%) | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Random Forest | **100.0** | All metrics are 100 |
| dNDVI differencing | 93.38 | |
| ForestUNet | 93.05 | False-positive rate **100%** |
| Siamese change detector | 81.22 | |

These numbers describe the generator, not real forests.

**What we take from it:**
- Its feature list is a good checklist of the expected solution.
- The τ slider and minimum-mapping-unit slider are the same idea as our filter controls.
- The road-encroachment buffer is close to our A2 (no-build zones).

**Where we beat it:**
- Real imagery.
- False alarms measured on no-change pairs.
- A reason and confidence per polygon.
- Context-aware severity.
- A mobile field loop.
- Honest metrics.

### P2. GeoAgent Monitor — real Sentinel-2, risk scoring, LLM agent

- **Repo:** https://github.com/Anil-sena/geo_monitoring_agent (last commit 2026-09-21)
- **What it does:** Sentinel-2 composites from **Google Earth Engine** → spectral change detection → **OpenStreetMap proximity scoring** (nearest infrastructure type, name and distance per polygon) → risk report. A **LangGraph agent** can drive the pipeline from natural language.
- **UI:** a Next.js 14 dashboard exported as static files and served by the same uvicorn process. Run reports have a **before/after slider**, a per-polygon risk table and GeoTIFF/GeoJSON downloads. There's a draw-an-area setting.
- **Change detection (verified, `pipeline/change_detection.py`):**
  - `SpectralChangeDetector` combines reflectance and NDVI differences, then standardises with a **median/MAD z-score**.
  - Threshold 0.35, minimum object 4 px.
  - Areas and distances are computed in the local UTM zone.
  - Author-reported: "896/900 px recovered on a synthetic change, 0 false positives on noise-only or identical inputs".
- **Checkpoints in the repo:** `models_store/eurosat.pt` (3.4 MB, a land-cover classifier) and `keras/cnn_scaler_9.joblib`.
- **Optional deep path:** commented out in `requirements.txt`; would use torch, segmentation-models-pytorch and **TorchGeo**.

**Tech stack:**

| Layer | Used |
|---|---|
| API | FastAPI ≥0.115, uvicorn / gunicorn, Pydantic v2, SSE, python-multipart |
| DB | SQLAlchemy 2 (SQLite in development, PostgreSQL via psycopg 3 in production) |
| Geo | earthengine-api, rasterio, GeoPandas, Shapely, pyproj, scikit-image, NumPy |
| Agent | LangGraph, langchain-core, langchain-openai, langchain-google-genai |
| Frontend | Next.js 14.2, React 18, Leaflet / React-Leaflet, react-markdown, Tailwind, TypeScript |
| Deploy | Dockerfile (python:3.11-slim + GDAL), systemd unit, nginx, install / update scripts |

**Datasets:**
- Sentinel-2 L2A composites (Earth Engine) and OpenStreetMap.
- EuroSAT for the land-cover checkpoint (inferred from the file name).
- Mentions LEVIR-CD and Sentinel-1.

**What we take from it:**
- The proximity-to-infrastructure attribute (like our A2/A3).
- The robust median/MAD normalisation.
- Running UI and API in one process.

**Its limits for GEOAI 04:**
- Needs an Earth Engine account.
- No Siamese model in the default path.
- No false-alarm measurement.

### P3. Aerial Change Intelligence — calibrated Siamese U-Net + India Sentinel-2 track

- **Repo:** https://github.com/bhavishyabondalapati/aerial-change-intelligence (last commit 2026-10-06)
- **Model:** a Siamese U-Net with a shared, ImageNet-pretrained **ResNet18** encoder. BCE + Dice loss; training augmentation includes random crops, flips and **before/after swaps**.
- **Author-reported results** on the **LEVIR-CD test split** (128 pairs, threshold 0.5, `docs/test_metrics.json`): precision 0.908, recall 0.889, **F1 0.898, IoU 0.815, expected calibration error 0.007**.
- **Geo:** mask → polygons → area in m² and hectares, using GeoPandas in a metric CRS.
- **Second track:** a Sentinel-2 NDVI vegetation-loss track over the **Godavari delta, India** (December 2024 vs March 2025), fetched with pystac-client from **Microsoft Planetary Computer** (no account), with cloud masking.
- **Tech stack:** PyTorch ≥2.4 (Apple `mps` device), torchvision, GeoPandas, Shapely, rasterio, pyproj, scikit-image, SciPy, pystac-client, planetary-computer, rioxarray, stackstac, pytest. Planned: google-genai (Gemini report), Streamlit.
- **Datasets:** LEVIR-CD from the Hugging Face mirror `satellite-image-deep-learning/LEVIR-CD` (≈2.4 GB, no login; **useful for us to compute full-test-set metrics**), and Sentinel-2 L2A. xBD is planned.
- **What we take from it:**
  - The LEVIR-CD Hugging Face mirror.
  - **Calibration (ECE)** to justify a per-polygon confidence.
  - Its own roadmap note: compare the *same* dry-season window across years so NDVI change isn't crop phenology. That's the same lesson as our same-season rule.

### P4. Satellite Bi-Temporal Change Detection — multi-dataset Siamese U-Net as an API

- **Repo:** https://github.com/abhay2367m/Change-Detection (last commit 2026-09-23)
- **Model:** a Siamese U-Net (ResNet-18 / 34 / 50 encoder) with |feat_A − feat_B| fusion at 5 pyramid levels, a bias-initialised output head, and BCE + Dice loss.
- **Key idea:** **multi-scale test-time fusion**. It runs at 30 / 40 / 60 / 80 / 100% scale and accepts a pixel when ≥2 of the 5 scales agree. It returns an `agreement_per_scale` value and an `"ok" / "check_by_eye"` verdict as a consistency signal.
- **Datasets:** **LEVIR-CD+** (985 pairs, Texas, about 0.5 m); **SYSU-CD** (20,000 pairs, Hong Kong); **JL1-CD** (5,000 pairs, Jilin-1, China). All are converted from Hugging Face releases.
- **Author-reported results** on LEVIR-CD+: validation F1 0.904; **test F1 0.798, IoU 0.664** at threshold 0.6.
- **Tech stack:** PyTorch ≥2.3, torchvision, NumPy, SciPy, Pillow (rasterio optional), served with **LitServe / FastAPI on Lightning AI**.
- **No weights in the repo.**
- **What we take from it:** the "check by eye" verdict is the same idea as our needs-review queue. Its finding that the model only resolves buildings 30–100 px across matters for how we tile deep mode.

### P7. Sentinel-2 Change Detection (Zambia mine) — clean classical pipeline on real data

- **Repo:** https://github.com/a-pradono/geo_change_detection (last commit 2026-09-16)
- **Pipeline:**
  1. AOI clip (pixels outside the AOI set to NaN before any statistic).
  2. Reflectance scaling.
  3. Radiometric normalisation.
  4. **PCA / CVA change magnitude**.
  5. NaN-safe **Otsu** threshold.
  6. **Morphological opening** to remove seam-line artefacts.
  7. Vectorise.
  8. **GeoPackage + SpatiaLite** database.
  9. A standalone Plotly HTML dashboard.
  10. A regression check against an expected percentage changed (`--expected-pct-changed 2.08`).
- **Tech stack:** rasterio, scikit-learn, scikit-image, GeoPandas, Shapely, rasterstats, Plotly, Pillow.
- **Dataset:** **real Sentinel-2 RGB (B02/B03/B04)** over an open-pit mine in Zambia, 2023-08-12 vs 2023-09-02.
- **What we take from it:** a good **classical baseline** to beat (PCA / CVA + Otsu), AOI-scoped statistics, and the regression test idea for our evaluation harness.

### 6.2 Gaps nobody has closed (our differentiation, backed by this survey)

1. **False alarms measured on real no-change / seasonal pairs.** Nobody reports false alarms per km² with the filter on and off. P1's metrics come from simulated scenes; P2's "0 false positives" claim is on synthetic noise.
2. **A reason per polygon.** No project explains *why* each polygon was kept. P2 has a per-run agent narrative, not per polygon.
3. **Context-aware severity** (no-build zones raise the tier, permits lower it, ward tags). Closest are P1's road buffer and P2's nearest-infrastructure distance.
4. **Field verification loop on a phone** (offline confirm/dismiss, navigate, field pack). None.
5. **Two engines auto-routed by resolution.** P1 has several models but trains all of them on one synthetic 10 m dataset; P3 runs two separate tracks; none routes by input resolution.
6. **Alignment check before detection.** None of the repos estimates and reports a global shift (from Goudemant et al.).
7. **Honest metrics on real imagery.** Several flagship-looking projects show simulated results (P1 data, P6 UI) or ship no weights (P4, P5).

## 7. Verification notes and limits of this survey

- Repos were checked at their latest commit on **2026-10-09**. They are active student/hackathon projects and may change.
- **Author-reported metrics were not re-run.** We only checked whether the data behind them is real or synthetic and whether weights exist.
- Licences were not reviewed. Check each licence before reusing code.
- The search was English-language and GitHub-centred. Products such as Google Earth Engine apps, Global Forest Watch and commercial platforms are covered in `docs/PROJECT_PLAN.md` §5, not here.
- The arXiv and SPJ pages could not be opened directly in the research conversation; their content came from the PDFs the research lead attached.
